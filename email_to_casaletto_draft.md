Separate from the RR9/SANS work, I've spent the past year developing a project of my own — a multi-omics reanalysis of the spaceflight mouse kidney that started from the public RRRM-2/OSD-771 dataset and has since grown into a full manuscript. I wanted to ask whether you think it's worth pursuing toward publication, and to get your read on it.

The short version: using the public mouse-kidney spaceflight datasets — including the matched RR-10/OSD-462 multi-omics from the Cosmic Kidney Disease study (Siew et al. 2024) — I find that the spaceflight kidney transporter signal isn't carried by transcript or total-protein abundance but at the regulatory phosphorylation of NCC and its WNK–SPAK/OSR1 kinases. The bulk-RNA response (matrix/endothelial remodeling, lower DCT/NCC-WNK transport) recurs across cohorts but doesn't propagate to protein. When I rank the parent genes of flight-suppressed phosphosites against an external DCT1/DCT2 single-nucleus prior, the suppression concentrates at distal-nephron subtype extremes — most robustly a DCT2/CNT-leaning bin, which would broaden the phenotype beyond the canonical NCC/DCT1 axis. An unbiased kinome-wide screen independently recovers the WNK arm.

Given our back-and-forth on the SANS notebooks, you'll appreciate that I tried to keep it tight on the methods: the analysis tiers are fixed before the flight contrasts, there's no outcome-based selection feeding the enrichment, and the whole thing is reproducible — figures and headline numbers regenerate from versioned run artifacts. I've also tried to be careful about what the data can't say: it's all public-data reanalysis, the phosphoproteomics is whole-kidney (so this is parent-gene prioritization, not cell-resolved localization), and I treat the result as hypothesis-generating, with segment-resolved phosphoproteomics as the proposed next experiment.

I'd really value your judgment on a few things:

- Whether the central result — the phosphorylation-layer transporter signal and the broadened DCT2/CNT reading — is novel and solid enough to take toward publication.
- What you'd prioritize to make it publisher-ready, and whether a venue like *npj Microgravity* or *Life Sciences in Space Research* seems like the right fit.
- Whether you'd be willing to look at the draft (and the repo) when you have a chance.
